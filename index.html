2 · Bihar Village Unit Plan (200 m² land)

Why Bihar works

  • Structurally undersupplied market — production never kept pace with demand; weak local competition.
  • Near-free inputs: paddy/wheat straw abundant; labour among India's cheapest.
  • 100-bag pilot costs ~₹30,000 in Bihar vs ~₹50,000 in NCR.

Species & Crop Calendar

SpeciesSeasonNotes
Oyster (Dhingri)Year-round workhorsePaddy/wheat straw, forgiving, 20–30 day cycles, 1–1.5 kg per kg dry substrate.
Paddy straw mushroomJun–Sep heat/humidityNear-zero-cost straw; strong eastern Bihar demand.
Button (Agaricus)Phase 2 onlyNeeds composting + pasteurisation + casing — higher capex/skill.

Infrastructure for 200 m²

  • ~93–120 m² shed (thatched/bamboo, mud walls) with cross-ventilation & 80–85% humidity.
  • Multi-layer bamboo/wood racks to multiply growing area 3–4×.
  • Drum boiler / steam pasteurisation setup.
  • Spawn from DMR / BAU Sabour / KVK; polythene bags; hygrometer + thermometer.
  • Remaining land: compost yard, drying area, future expansion.

Sales Channels (Bihar)

  1. Patna hotels/restaurants — Bailey Road & Boring Road corridors pay the highest direct-to-buyer prices.
  2. Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Gaya mandis & retailers.
  3. Eastern districts → Kolkata overnight truck market (higher price tier).
  4. Nepal border trade — see Page 4.

3 · Bangalore Unit Plan

The opposite play: high costs, huge premium HoReCa demand. Position as fresh, local, chemical-free gourmet supplier.

Setup Steps

  1. Training: ICAR-IIHR Mushroom Lab (mushroom.iihr.res.in) — training + Ready-to-Fruit incubation facility. Jan batches prioritise out-of-state candidates. Alternatives: GKVK/UAS, ShroomGarten (Bommasandra).
  2. Species: oyster varieties, king oyster, shiitake, milky — exotics fetch ₹1,200–1,500/kg in premium markets vs commodity button.
  3. Site: peri-urban 300–500 bag unit (Hosur Road, Bommasandra, Devanahalli belt); humidity control (foggers) for summer.
  4. Licences: FSSAI + trade licence; GST at scale.

Sales Channels (Bangalore)

  1. Direct B2B: hotels on MG Road/UB City/Whitefield — walk in with samples + weekly supply commitment + WhatsApp ordering.
  2. QSR distribution: via distributors like Zomato Hyperpure, BigBasket seller program.
  3. Cloud kitchens & chefs: reliability over price; grow via chef referrals.
  4. D2C: subscription boxes, farmers' markets.
  5. Phase 2 export: IEC + APEDA RCMC → Nepal/UAE (dried).
Certifications that win hotel contracts: FSSAI first, then HACCP/ISO 22000 — premium buyers specifically demand these.

4 · Nepal Export & Hotel/Food-Chain Sales Playbook

Option A — Border trade (start here)

  • Use land customs stations: East Champaran → Raxaul/Birgunj, Sitamarhi/Madhubani → Jaynagar, Araria → Jogbani.
  • Kathmandu hotels/retailers source Indian vegetables heavily through Birgunj — get a local commission agent; no export paperwork at small volumes.
  • Fresh target: Birgunj–Kathmandu corridor (≤1 day transit).

Option B — Formal export (phase 2)

  1. IEC from DGFT (free, online, lifetime).
  2. FSSAI licence.
  3. APEDA RCMC via DGFT e-RCMC portal (₹5,000 + GST, 5 yrs).
  4. Phytosanitary certificate (PQMS) per consignment at border/LCS.
  5. GST + current account + AD code at bank.
  6. Add dried mushrooms (oyster/shiitake) for shelf life & higher value/kg.

Hotel / Food-Chain Sales Playbook

ElementStandard
ProductConsistent sizing, trimmed stems, food-grade punnets (200g/500g), harvested 2–3 days before full maturity.
Cold chainCrates + ice gel packs; 2–4°C, shelf life 3–5 days.
Entry tacticFree samples within 24h of harvest + rate card + fixed weekly commitment. Start with 5–10 kitchens.
PricingSlight discount to retail at volume; exotics at premium. HoReCa absorbs ~40% of metro mushroom volume.
DiversificationNever depend on one chain — hotels + QSR distributors + retail + export.
Reality check: Nepal's formal imports from India are ~233 tonnes/year total. Good money for a border village unit, but it's a van-and-LCS business, not a container business.
